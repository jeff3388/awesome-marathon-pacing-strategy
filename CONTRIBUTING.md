# Contributing to Awesome Marathon Pacing Strategy

Thank you for contributing. This list aims to be the most evidence-based, practically useful resource on marathon pacing strategy available on the open web.

## What We Accept

- Peer-reviewed research on pacing, fatigue, and performance in distance running
- Evidence-based coaching frameworks from credentialed sources
- Tools and calculators that apply verified pacing models
- Race analysis and datasets that illuminate pacing patterns at scale
- Books and courses from authors who cite primary research

## What We Do Not Accept

- Content from brands promoting specific products
- Anecdotal accounts without broader supporting evidence
- Affiliate or sponsored content
- Paywalled resources without clear indication of access limitations

## Submission Format

```markdown
- [Resource Title](https://url.example.com) — One concise sentence describing what it provides and why it belongs here.
```

Place submissions in the most relevant section. If no section fits, suggest a new one in your pull request description.

## Evidence Standard

Where research is cited, include the primary author and year (e.g., Smyth 2018, Ely 2007). Distinguish between:

- **Established consensus** — multiple independent studies, replicated findings
- **Emerging evidence** — promising early research, small samples, or single studies
- **Practitioner frameworks** — experience-based models not yet formally tested

Label accordingly if adding content in the latter two categories.

## Pacing-Specific Guidance

This list focuses on marathon pacing specifically. Submissions about general running training, nutrition, or fitness belong in more appropriate awesome-lists. The focus here is the intersection of physiology, strategy, and execution on race day and in the weeks before.

## Pull Request Process

1. Fork the repository
2. Add your resource to the appropriate section
3. Submit a pull request with a brief description of what the resource adds and why it meets the evidence standard

All submissions are reviewed for relevance, evidence quality, and link validity before merging.
